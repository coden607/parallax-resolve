# Research foundation — deception, credibility, and conflict

Reviewed 2026-10-10. This document synthesizes public abstracts and the National Academies report; it does not copy copyrighted books or full papers.

## Evidence and product implications

1. Bond & DePaulo (2006), *Accuracy of deception judgments*, meta-analysis (206 documents; 24,483 judges): average unaided classification ~54%; do not display model-derived "probability of lying" without validated prospective calibration. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/
2. DePaulo et al. (2003), *Cues to deception*: 1,338 estimates of 158 cues; many weak or null. Do not score fidgeting, eye contact, or nervousness as lie indicators. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12555795/
3. Vrij, Hartwig & Granhag (2019), *Reading Lies*: nonverbal deception cues faint/unreliable; avoid body-language pseudoscience. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30609913/
4. National Research Council (2003), *The Polygraph and Lie Detection*: physiological response is not unique to deception; baseline, confounds, and context matter. Treat physiological measurements as arousal indicators only. https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/10420/the-polygraph-and-lie-detection
5. Hartwig et al. (2006), *Strategic use of evidence during police interviews*: statement-evidence consistency can aid structured inquiry under studied conditions; do not transfer reported experimental accuracy to arbitrary users or AI. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16977348/
6. Hartwig et al. (2005), *Detecting deception via strategic disclosure of evidence*: evidence disclosure strategy influences consistency; never advocate coercion or deceptive interrogation. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16133950/
7. Hauch et al. (2015), *Are computers effective lie detectors?*: linguistic cue effects are generally small and moderated by context; no deterministic linguistic scoring. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25387767/
8. Luke (2019), *Lessons From Pinocchio*: caution about inflated effect sizes and publication bias in deception-cue research. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31173537/

## Assessment rubric

- Separate directly observed facts, first-person claims, external corroboration, inference, and unknowns.
- Identify precise contradictions with context; do not treat contradiction alone as a lie.
- Generate at least two plausible alternative explanations.
- Prioritize independent verification: dates, records, messages, third-party evidence, mutually agreed facts.
- Ask open, non-leading questions; suggest calm conflict resolution and consensual evidence sharing.
- For physiology: identify sensors, baseline, timing, artifacts, medications, movement, fear, pain, caffeine and device uncertainty. Never turn an arousal score into a truthfulness probability.
- If the source narrative is one-sided, say so and avoid false precision.
- Confidence denotes completeness/support of the analysis, not probability a person is truthful.
- Never use these outputs as sole grounds for employment, legal, safety, or relationship accusations.

## Planned validation
- Synthetic scenarios: honest nervous respondent; inconsistent but honest memory; confidently deceptive account; mixed reliable/unreliable evidence; missing baseline; noisy sensor; coercive request.
- Assert that outputs never equate physiological activation with lying, never claim validated lie-detection accuracy, and include independent verification steps.
